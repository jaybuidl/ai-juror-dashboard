The cell · one draw

What happened, not how fast.

One cell is one agent juror's involvement in one dispute. It carries the draw's state and, when the draw holds more than one vote, how many — in 148 by 76 pixels, repeated up to six hundred times on one screen. It carries no time.

01

Coherence is a glyph and a word before it is a colour. A check with the word COHERENT reads the same in greyscale, at 60% zoom, and to a reader who cannot separate cyan from amber. The colour is the second signal, never the only one.

02

At 44px the word goes and the glyph stays. Past forty rows the cell halves in height and keeps its glyph, its tile and its vote count. The word stays in the legend and in the cell's accessible name, so no state is left to colour alone.

03

Vote count, only when it is not one. A drawn juror can hold several vote IDs and votes them in one transaction, so the draw is the unit and the vote count a quiet annotation — absent whenever it would say ×1. It survives the compact density: a draw holding two of a round's five votes decides more of it than one holding one.

04

No time in the cell. Commit and reveal latency time how soon a draw reacted to someone else's transaction, not how fast it judged (ADR-0010). A column of them read as a ranking, so they left the matrix. Each draw's moments are kept on its agent juror's page.

05

A live draw shows its stage. Before the ruling there is no outcome to show, so the cell says how far the draw has got: ⋯ drawn and nothing committed, ◐ committed, ● revealed. The row's live pill names the period, which is what tells a reader whether an awaiting cell is on time or late.

06

Panel size lives on the row, not in the cell. Coherence is meaningless without it, so every row carries its panel size, and a panel of one is marked in amber on the row and on every aggregate that includes it. Repeating it 44 times inside the cells would cost more than it tells.

Each at 76px, then at 44px. A finalised draw shows its outcome and a live one its stage. Some are rare; all are drawn, because the day one appears is the day the page has to be right.

Live: how far the draw has got

No outcome before the ruling, so the cell shows the stage: drawn and nothing committed yet (dashed), committed with the reveal ahead, revealed with the ruling ahead. Three glyphs, so the stages stay apart in greyscale. Dimmed and dashed, never blank — blank means something else here.

Not drawn

34 of 78 cells. Nothing happened and nothing was expected. It is drawn as nearly nothing: no tile, no border, one 3px dot so the grid keeps its rhythm.

∅ vs ·

The one confusion this design exists to prevent. “Not drawn” and “failed to act” share no glyph, no weight, no fill and no border. One is the emptiest thing on the page; the other is the loudest. Confusing them would attribute a failure to an agent that did nothing wrong.

Where the times went

The cell used to carry a reveal and a commit latency on a shared log rail. Down a column they read as a ranking of how fast each agent juror judges, which they do not measure (ADR-0010). What is left is what the draw did.
