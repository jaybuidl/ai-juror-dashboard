The cell · one draw

Five states that must never be confused.

One cell is one agent juror's involvement in one dispute. It carries two latencies, a coherence state, a vote count and the panel context it depends on — in 148 by 76 pixels, repeated up to six hundred times on one screen.

01

Coherence is a glyph and a word before it is a colour. A check with the word COHERENT reads the same in greyscale, at 60% zoom, and to a reader who cannot separate cyan from amber. The colour is the second signal, never the only one.

02

Reveal latency is the headline number. It is the figure the experiment is about, so it is the largest thing in the cell and the only one in heading ink. Values under two minutes read in seconds; longer ones switch to minutes, because 3,236s is not a number anyone reads.

03

Vote count, only when it is not one. A drawn juror can hold several vote IDs and votes them in one transaction. 61 votes collapse to 44 draws, so the draw is the unit and the vote count is a quiet annotation — absent whenever it would say ×1.

04

Commit latency sits below, dimmer. Same unit, same scale, half the weight. It is context for the reveal, not a competing figure — and it is the one number the dispute-151 window change makes incomparable, which is why it never appears as a percentage.

05

One log rail, two marks. The data spans three orders of magnitude: 7 seconds to 54 minutes. A linear bar would render every fast draw as nothing at all. The rail is logarithmic from 1s to 1h, shared by both measures so they can be compared by eye, and it is decoration only — the number is always present.

06

Panel size lives on the row, not in the cell. Coherence is meaningless without it, so every row carries its panel size, and a panel of one is marked in amber on the row and on every aggregate that includes it. Repeating it 44 times inside the cells would cost more than it tells.

Not drawn

34 of 78 cells. Nothing happened and nothing was expected. It is drawn as nearly nothing: no tile, no border, one 3px dot so the grid keeps its rhythm.

∅ vs ·

The one confusion this design exists to prevent. “Not drawn” and “failed to act” share no glyph, no weight, no fill and no border. One is the emptiest thing on the page; the other is the loudest. Confusing them would attribute a failure to an agent that did nothing wrong.

Why the rail is logarithmic

Reveal latency runs from 7s to 9m 12s; commit latency from 2m 06s to 53m 56s. On a linear scale the fastest three quarters of the record would be indistinguishable from zero.
